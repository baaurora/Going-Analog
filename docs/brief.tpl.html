01
The
Assessment

There is already a brand here. It is stronger than you think.

Nine seconds of footage and one Google Form contain a complete identity: two marks, a photographic language, a typeface, a voice, and a ritual. Almost nothing needs inventing. It needs deciding.

The marks

Two pieces of identity are already locked, and they appear above exactly as the film sets them. White, all caps, centered, on true black. No logo, no icon, no rules. That restraint is the single biggest asset and it should survive contact with the website.

GO.A807 reads as GOING ANALOG plus a date code, and you have confirmed it rolls: the current mark is GO.A0925. That makes the wordmark a serial number rather than a logo, which is the single best structural idea in the brand. It means the identity is dated the way a roll of film is dated, it can never go stale, and every past event keeps its own mark instead of being absorbed into a generic one.

Two consequences for the build. The mark is a variable, not an image, so it gets set live in type and swapped by editing one string. And the site needs somewhere for retired marks to live, which is what makes the About timeline load-bearing rather than decorative.

This is disposable-camera flash photography and it does all the emotional work. Faces are laughing, mid-sentence, mid-peace-sign. Nobody is performing for a feed. The design system's only job is to get out of its way, which is the entire argument for a white page and black type.

Typography in the film

An industrial condensed grotesque in the DIN family, set two ways. Spurred G, flat-topped 7, tight geometric 8, flat terminals, straight tick apostrophes, straight-legged R. The tagline is lighter and more condensed with wide letterspacing. The wordmark is heavier, wider, tightly tracked. It reads as a stencil on a road case, not a startup logo. This entire document is set in that face so you can judge it at length.

What actually characterizes it: direct address, second person, with a real person audibly behind it rather than a brand. Comma splices and run-ons, because it is typed like a text and not written like a manifesto. Parenthetical asides that undercut anything sincere. Profanity is load-bearing, and sanding it off turns this into a wellness retreat. It never moralizes about screen time, because the bit is “we are all cooked, so let's hang,” not “digital detox.”

Banned in all copy for this brand
  • No rhetorical triplets. Three parallel examples in a row is the single loudest AI tell. Use one, or two, and then stop.
  • No metaphors, no analogies, no imagery. This brand does not reach for a picture to make a point. It just says the thing.
  • No aphorisms. If a sentence would look at home cross-stitched on a pillow, cut it. “The part we forgot was the point” is exactly the failure.
  • No em-dashes and no semicolons, per your standing rule.
  • Never explain the thesis earnestly. The anti-phone position only ever arrives as a joke. The moment it argues for itself it becomes a digital detox retreat.

Test for any line: would Judy text this? If it reads like it was composed, it is wrong.

The ritual is the product

Phones are confiscated at the door. Everything else, the film cameras and the delayed photos and this website, is downstream of one physical act at a threshold. Which is why your phone-jail idea is not a gimmick. It is the only part of the party a website can actually reproduce, and it should be the first thing that happens.

Money and cause need to sit on the page plainly, without a fundraising-page tone: sliding scale, $100 AI-startup tier, proceeds to the Nepal Prime Minister's Disaster Relief Fund.

The one real tension, and how the build resolves it

The film brand is black, nocturnal, industrial, DIN. The references you sent are white, Swiss, editorial, paper. Right now those are two different brands.

The resolution: white is daylight, black is the party. The site is the archive, the morning after, the contact sheet on the studio wall. It is white paper and black type. Black appears in exactly two places, the entry gate and the photographs themselves. The flash photos become the only color on the page, and every time black floods the screen it means something is happening. This document is built on that rule, which is why it opens on two black cards and then turns to paper.

Type

Locked: Archivo, and only Archivo. It is free under the Open Font License, it is variable with both a weight and a width axis, and that one family covers the condensed tagline and the bold wordmark without mixing anything. No paid tier, no second family, nothing to license. The font files get committed into the repo and self-hosted rather than loaded from Google's CDN, so the site keeps working offline, loads faster, and never leaks visitors to a third party. That last part matters for a group whose entire thesis is not being surveilled.

One note on this document specifically. It is set in DIN Condensed, which ships free with macOS and is almost exactly the face in the film. That is why the specimen above matches so closely. Do not use it for the website, because visitors on Windows and Android do not have it and would silently fall back to something wrong. Use it freely for anything you make locally in Figma, InDesign, or Canva.

Motion

Slow, physical, weighted. Polaroids get inertia and settle rather than snapping. Nothing eases out with a bounce. With the cursor follower cut, motion now lives in three places only: the gate unlocking, the polaroid flip, and the nav fanning open. Restraint here is what separates this from every AI-generated party site.

Entry gate, before the site

Black screen. The cursor is a smartphone. Drag it into the phone jail to unlock. On release the screen flips from black to white and the site is behind it. Mobile has no cursor, so mobile becomes a tap-and-drag of the phone into the box, or a press-and-hold “surrender your phone” button.

Home

White. Left half is a physical pile of polaroids you flip through by hand, one at a time, with rotation and drop shadow. Right half is a large block of black text, Samson Leung structure but black rather than red: the name, then the thesis, one dense column with hanging indents.

The pile — received and processed

Judy's folder had 23 lab scans off 7 rolls, 3637×2444 TIFFs, delivered 11 August, three days after the party. All 23 are downloaded and converted to web JPEGs in ~/going-analog/photos, 4.6MB for the whole set. Nothing is blocking the build anymore.

Framing decision, and it is not Polaroid. These are 35mm, and 35mm is 3:2. Forcing every frame into a square Polaroid crop cuts people out of the wide group shots, including the entire room shot on R2-025A. But the square crop does look punchier on the tight portraits, so the pile carries both shapes: square-cropped prints for the portraits and centered groups, 3:2 prints for anything wide. A real pile of prints from a lab is not uniform, and the mixed shapes are what will make it read as physical objects instead of a grid of identical cards.

Pile order, strongest first. R7-000A opens it, because a man photographing you with a disposable camera is the entire thesis in one frame. R5-012A is the same idea from the other side. Then R4-009A, R2-018A, R4-016A, R6-023A, R1-000A, R3-019A, R5-010A, R4-012A, R2-003A, R5-019A, R2-025A, R2-020A, R7-006A, R3-018A. That is 16. The remaining 7 are underexposed or soft and are better held back than padded in.

One thing worth raising once and then dropping: roughly forty identifiable people are in these frames, and the site puts them on the open web. The brand's own line is that the photos go here and nowhere else, so publishing is the point. Just worth Judy having said it out loud to the group rather than assumed.

Navigation

Semi-hidden, Studio Dogu style. A small stack of slightly rotated labels tucked in a corner that fans out on hover. Three items, all scrolling to sections on one page rather than routing anywhere: Home, RSVP, About.

What to ask ChatGPT for, and what not to

Read this before you generate anything

Do not ask it to put polaroid borders on your real party photos. Image models redraw faces when they re-render a photo, and your friends will come back looking like slightly wrong strangers. This is now settled: I have the 23 originals, and the frames, rotation, shadow and flip get built in code from the untouched scans, sharp at any screen size. Nothing about the pile needs to go near an image model.

Do not ask it to cut objects out of your real photos either. macOS does that for free. Right click the image in Finder, then Quick Actions, then Remove Background.

Do ask it for the invented objects. Everything in the table below is generated art, which is what these models are genuinely good at.

The house style block — paste at the end of every object prompt

Photographic product shot, not an illustration. Single object, centered, generous margin. Shot on a 50mm lens, shallow depth of field, studio softbox key light from the upper left, one soft contact shadow beneath the object. Slightly desaturated, mild film grain, very slightly warm white balance. Pure transparent background, no scene, no surface, no text, no watermarks, no logos, no hands, no people. Square 1:1, high resolution.

That block is what makes twelve separately generated files look like one set instead of a clip-art bin. It is not optional. If you only have appetite for a few, generate A1, A2, A7 and A8 first, since those are the four the site cannot open without.

06
Open
Questions

Five decisions still open

  1. Zero-padding convention. You wrote 0925, but 807 was not padded. If the rule is MMDD then August was really GO.A0807, and a November 4 event would be GO.A1104. If the rule is unpadded then September should read GO.A925. I need one rule, because it governs every future mark. My recommendation is MMDD, always four digits, since it stays sorted and never reads ambiguously.
  2. Does 807 get retconned? Either the August mark stays GO.A807 as a first-edition quirk, which is charming and I would keep it, or it becomes GO.A0807 for consistency. Also worth resolving: 807 reads as August 7, but your timeline says the party was August 8.
  3. Is the public name “Going Analog” or “GO.A”? The film only ever shows GO.A807. The full name has never actually appeared anywhere.
  4. Mobile entry gate. There is no cursor on a phone. Tap-and-drag, press-and-hold, or should mobile skip the gate and go straight in?
  5. Should the gate be skippable? A hard gate in front of all content loses some people and is bad for anyone on a screen reader. My recommendation is a small skip that fades in after about eight seconds.
  6. Where does this deploy? GitHub Pages under your account like Kitty Corner and Pride Weekend, or does Judy need to own hosting too?
  7. Whose project is this, socially? The form and the response data already sit in Judy's Drive, which is the right call. The open half is the repo and the domain. My recommendation is that those start under Judy too, because migrating a live domain after people have the link is the kind of chore that quietly kills a Volume Three.
